INDIAN FREEDOM STRUGGLE
1857 – 1900: The Awakening
1857
The First War of Independence (Sepoy Mutiny)
The first major organized resistance against British East India Company rule. It started in
Meerut and spread across Northern and Central India, becoming a symbol of resistance.
1858
Government of India Act
The British Crown took direct control of India from the East India Company, marking the
beginning of the British Raj.
1885
Formation of the Indian National Congress (INC)
Founded by Allan Octavian Hume, the INC provided a platform for educated Indians to seek
political representation and civil rights.
1905 – 1918: Radicalism and the Return of Gandhi
1905
Partition of Bengal & Swadeshi Movement
Lord Curzon's decision to partition Bengal on communal lines sparked massive protests and
the Swadeshi movement, which promoted Indian-made goods.
1906
A Chronological Journey: 1857 – 1950

Formation of the All India Muslim League
Established in Dhaka to safeguard the rights of Muslims in India and voice their political
aspirations.
1915
Return of Mahatma Gandhi
Mahatma Gandhi returned from South Africa, bringing his philosophy of Satyagraha (truth-
force) and non-violent resistance to the forefront.
1916
Lucknow Pact
A historic agreement between the Indian National Congress and the Muslim League to work
together for constitutional reforms.
1919 – 1939: The Era of Mass Movements
1919
Jallianwala Bagh Massacre & Rowlatt Act
British troops fired on a peaceful gathering in Amritsar. This atrocity followed the repressive
Rowlatt Act and became a turning point in the struggle.
1920
Non-Cooperation Movement
Launched by Gandhi to demand Purna Swaraj (self-rule). Millions boycotted British
institutions, titles, and goods.
1922
Chauri Chaura Incident
Following violence in Chauri Chaura, Gandhi suspended the Non-Cooperation Movement,
emphasizing that non-violence was paramount.

-- 2 of 4 --

1928
Simon Commission Protests
Massive protests with the slogan "Simon Go Back" took place against the all-British
commission sent to propose reforms.
1930
Salt Satyagraha (Dandi March)
Gandhi's 240-mile march to produce salt in defiance of the British salt tax, sparking the Civil
Disobedience Movement.
1935
Government of India Act
Introduced provincial autonomy and provided for a federation of India, though it was largely
criticized by Indian leaders.
1940 – 1947: The Final Push
1940
Lahore Resolution
The Muslim League formally raised the demand for a separate nation, Pakistan.
1942
Quit India Movement
Gandhi's call to the British to "Quit India" and to Indians to "Do or Die." This led to the arrest of
almost the entire INC leadership.
1943
Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj)
Subhash Chandra Bose led the INA to liberate India from British rule with help from the Axis
powers during WWII.

-- 3 of 4 --

1947 (Aug 15)
Independence and Partition
India achieved independence but was partitioned into India and Pakistan, accompanied by
mass displacement and violence.
1948 – 1950: Building the Republic
1948
Integration of Princely States
Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel spearheaded the integration of over 560 princely states into the
Indian Union.
1949 (Nov 26)
Adoption of the Constitution
The Constituent Assembly of India adopted the Constitution, the result of nearly three years of
deliberation.
1950 (Jan 26)
India Becomes a Republic
The Constitution came into force, establishing India as a Sovereign Democratic Republic. Dr.
Rajendra Prasad was sworn in as the first President.
