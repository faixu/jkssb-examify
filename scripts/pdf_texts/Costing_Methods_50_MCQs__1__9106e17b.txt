Costing Methods: 50 MCQ Practice Bank
Marginal, Absorption, Standard, and Uniform Costing
Section A: Marginal Costing
1. In marginal costing, which costs are treated as product costs?
A. Fixed costs only
B. Variable costs only
C. Both fixed and variable costs
D. Semi-variable costs only
Answer: B
2. Marginal costing is also known as:
A. Direct costing
B. Absorption costing
C. Historical costing
D. Standard costing
Answer: A
3. Contribution is equal to:
A. Sales − Fixed Costs
B. Sales − Variable Costs
C. Fixed Costs + Profit
D. Both B and C
Answer: D
4. Under marginal costing, inventory is valued at:
A. Total cost
B. Fixed production cost
C. Variable production cost
D. Market price
Answer: C
5. Marginal cost is the change in total cost resulting from producing:
A. A batch of products
B. One additional unit of output
C. A new product line
D. None of the above
Answer: B
6. Which of the following is true about Fixed Costs in marginal costing?
A. They are apportioned to products
B. They are treated as period costs and charged to the Profit & Loss account
C. They are carried forward to the next period as inventory
D. They are ignored completely
Answer: B

8. If sales are Rs. 100,000, variable costs are Rs. 60,000, and fixed costs are Rs. 30,000, the profit under
marginal costing is:
A. Rs. 40,000
B. Rs. 10,000
C. Rs. 70,000
D. Rs. 30,000
Answer: B
9. Marginal costing assumes that all costs can be classified into:
A. Direct and indirect costs
B. Fixed and variable costs
C. Controllable and uncontrollable costs
D. Normal and abnormal costs
Answer: B
10. In a period where production exactly equals sales, the profit calculated under marginal costing
compared to absorption costing will be:
A. Higher
B. Lower
C. Equal
D. Unpredictable
Answer: C
11. A sunk cost is:
A. Relevant for marginal costing decisions
B. A future cost that differs between alternatives
C. A historical cost that cannot be changed
D. A variable cost
Answer: C
12. The Profit/Volume (P/V) ratio is unaffected by changes in:
A. Selling price per unit
B. Variable cost per unit
C. Total fixed costs
D. Sales mix
Answer: C
13. In a make-or-buy decision using marginal costing, a company should 'make' if:
A. Marginal cost of making is less than the purchase price
B. Total cost of making is less than the purchase price
C. Fixed costs are high
D. The purchase price equals marginal cost
Answer: A
14. Which statement is false regarding marginal costing?
A. It avoids arbitrary apportionment of fixed overheads
B. It is highly suitable for external financial reporting
C. It highlights the contribution of each product
D. It simplifies the valuation of stock

Section B: Absorption Costing
16. Absorption costing is also known as:
A. Variable costing
B. Direct costing
C. Full costing
D. Marginal costing
Answer: C
17. Under absorption costing, fixed manufacturing overheads are:
A. Treated as period costs
B. Ignored in product valuation
C. Absorbed into the cost of units produced
D. Charged directly to the Profit & Loss account
Answer: C
18. When production is greater than sales (inventory increases), the profit under absorption costing will
be:
A. Higher than marginal costing profit
B. Lower than marginal costing profit
C. Equal to marginal costing profit
D. Zero
Answer: A
19. Which of the following items is included in inventory valuation under absorption costing but
excluded under marginal costing?
A. Direct Materials
B. Direct Labor
C. Variable Manufacturing Overheads
D. Fixed Manufacturing Overheads
Answer: D
20. The problem of over or under-absorption of overheads arises only in:
A. Marginal Costing
B. Standard Costing
C. Absorption Costing
D. Uniform Costing
Answer: C
21. In absorption costing, gross profit is calculated by deducting:
A. Variable costs from sales
B. Total manufacturing costs (fixed + variable) from sales
C. Fixed costs from sales
D. Non-manufacturing costs from sales
Answer: B
22. Which costing method is generally required for external reporting and tax purposes (e.g.,
GAAP/IFRS)?
A Marginal Costing

24. Fixed production overheads are absorbed based on a predetermined rate. If actual hours worked are
less than budgeted, it typically results in:
A. Over-absorption of overheads
B. Under-absorption of overheads
C. No effect on overheads
D. Increase in fixed costs
Answer: B
25. If sales exceed production (inventory decreases), absorption costing profit will be:
A. Higher than marginal costing profit
B. Lower than marginal costing profit
C. Equal to marginal costing profit
D. Unchanged
Answer: B
Section C: Standard Costing
26. Standard costing is a technique of:
A. Cost ascertainment
B. Cost reduction
C. Cost control
D. Cost allocation
Answer: C
27. A standard cost is a:
A. Historical cost
B. Predetermined cost representing what a product *should* cost
C. Sunk cost
D. Variable cost only
Answer: B
28. The difference between standard cost and actual cost is known as:
A. Profit
B. Variance
C. Margin
D. Contribution
Answer: B
29. Material Price Variance is calculated as:
A. (Standard Price − Actual Price) × Actual Quantity
B. (Standard Price − Actual Price) × Standard Quantity
C. (Standard Quantity − Actual Quantity) × Standard Price
D. (Standard Quantity − Actual Quantity) × Actual Price
Answer: A
30. An adverse (unfavorable) variance occurs when:
A. Actual cost < Standard cost
B. Actual cost > Standard cost
C Standard cost = Actual cost

32. Labor Rate Variance is equal to:
A. (Standard Hours − Actual Hours) × Standard Rate
B. (Standard Rate − Actual Rate) × Actual Hours
C. (Standard Rate − Actual Rate) × Standard Hours
D. (Standard Hours − Actual Hours) × Actual Rate
Answer: B
33. Which variance arises due to workers taking more time than allowed to complete a job?
A. Labor Rate Variance
B. Labor Mix Variance
C. Labor Efficiency Variance
D. Labor Yield Variance
Answer: C
34. A 'Basic Standard' is:
A. A standard established for use over a long period
B. A standard based on ideal conditions
C. A standard that is updated monthly
D. A standard based solely on historical averages
Answer: A
35. Idle Time Variance is always:
A. Favorable
B. Adverse
C. Zero
D. Equal to efficiency variance
Answer: B
36. If Standard Cost = Rs. 1,000 and Actual Cost = Rs. 1,200, the variance is:
A. Rs. 200 Favorable
B. Rs. 200 Adverse
C. Rs. 2,200
D. Rs. 1,000
Answer: B
37. Standard costing is most suitable for:
A. Job order industries
B. Process industries with repetitive mass production
C. Service industries like hospitals
D. Custom contract businesses
Answer: B
38. Overhead Volume Variance is a part of:
A. Variable Overhead Variance
B. Fixed Overhead Variance
C. Material Variance
D. Labor Variance
Answer: B

40. Material Cost Variance is the sum of:
A. Material Price Variance + Material Usage Variance
B. Material Mix Variance + Material Yield Variance
C. Labor Rate Variance + Material Price Variance
D. Fixed Overhead + Variable Overhead

Section D: Uniform Costing
41. Uniform costing is:
A. A distinct, separate method of costing like process costing
B. The use of the same costing principles by several undertakings in the same industry
C. Costing applied only to uniform, identical products
D. A technique used only in monopolies
Answer: B
42. The main purpose of uniform costing is to:
A. Increase competition blindly
B. Facilitate inter-firm comparison
C. Avoid paying taxes
D. Standardize selling prices across all industries
Answer: B
43. Which of the following is essential for the success of uniform costing?
A. Mutual trust and cooperation among participating firms
B. All firms must be of the exact same size
C. Government mandate
D. Zero variance in standard costs
Answer: A
44. A document providing comprehensive guidelines for uniform costing to participating firms is called
a:
A. Cost Sheet
B. Cost Manual
C. Standard Ledger
D. Master Budget
Answer: B
45. Inter-firm comparison involves comparing:
A. Historical costs with standard costs of a single firm
B. Financial and cost performance of different firms in an industry
C. Actual costs with budgeted costs
D. Marginal cost with absorption cost
Answer: B
46. Uniform costing is extremely helpful in:
A. Negotiating with trade unions and government
B. Hiding inefficiencies
C Eliminating fixed costs entirely

48. A major limitation of uniform costing is that:
A. It creates unhealthy competition
B. Firms may be hesitant to share confidential cost data
C. It violates accounting standards
D. It is only applicable to small-scale industries
Answer: B
49. Inter-firm comparison is often facilitated by:
A. A central trade association or independent body
B. The lowest performing firm
C. Shareholders of the individual firms
D. Creditors
Answer: A
50. To make inter-firm comparison meaningful, it is highly recommended to use:
A. Ratios instead of absolute figures
B. Only absolute profit figures
C. Different accounting periods for different firms
D. Historical costs from a decade ago
Answer: A
